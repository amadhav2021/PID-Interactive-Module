Analysis

We did it! This last piece is called the integral controller, since it keeps track of the accumulated error over time. Its corresponding integral gain (KI) determines how much this part contributes to the overall control. Together with the proportional and derivative controllers, the drone now reaches the target precisely and hovers there. This strategy is known as a PID controller and is one of the most common controllers for closed feedback systems. These systems are everywhere: thermostats, self-driving cars, industrial processes, and yes, even drones!

Gain tuning is an important part of control engineering. By adjusting each gain, you can affect factors like overshooting, time taken to reach the target, and stability. Try it out above and see how the drone reacts to gain changes. Once the drone finds a stable equilibrium, try changing its target to see how it reacts to a different set point. Right now, we’re just manually updating the gains, but there are mathematical ways to precisely tune gains based on system requirements.
